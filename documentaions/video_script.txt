# Video Script — Interactive Visualization Platform for Informatics Education Statistics
# Target duration: ~4:00 minutes
# 10 slides · delivery style: clear, conversational, academic — not a sales pitch

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
SLIDE 1 — Title  (~20 sec)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Good afternoon — my name is Břetislav Milota, and together with my colleague Martin Schön
we built an interactive web platform for exploring informatics education
statistics across European universities.

This was our semester project for Information Visualization —
at the University of West Bohemia in Pilsen.

[pause 1 sec]

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
SLIDE 2 — The Problem & Goal  (~30 sec)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The problem we set out to solve is this:

Informatics Europe — the association of CS and informatics
institutions across Europe — maintains multi-year data on student
enrollment, graduation rates, and female representation.

But all of that data lives in Excel spreadsheets.
Finding a geographic pattern, comparing two countries, or spotting a
14-year trend requires a significant amount of work.

Our goal was to turn those spreadsheets into a unified,
interactive, browser-based platform — usable by researchers,
administrators, and policy makers — covering over 200 institutions
across 34 countries with 14 years of data.

[pause 1 sec]

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
SLIDE 3 — Interactive Map  (~30 sec)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The main entry point is a full-screen interactive map.

At a continent-wide zoom, markers cluster automatically to show
institutional density. As you zoom in, clusters expand to reveal
individual universities — and in countries with detailed data,
you can drill down to individual departments.

Color coding keeps things readable: green markers are IE member
institutions, blue markers are general institutions, and a selected
marker turns red.

Selecting any institution from the search bar triggers a smooth
fly-to animation, and opens a detail modal with its full statistical profile.

[pause 1 sec]

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
SLIDE 4 — Choropleth Layer  (~35 sec)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Overlaid on the map is a configurable choropleth layer.

You can choose any combination of degree level — Bachelor's, Master's,
or PhD — with enrollment type, and then pick a metric: total count,
female count, female share as a percentage, or count per million inhabitants.

These two maps illustrate the point well.
On the left: absolute female Bachelor's enrollment in 2010.
On the right: the same metric in 2023.

The colors have shifted — absolute numbers clearly grew.
But when you switch to the female share metric, the picture barely moves.
Thirteen years of data, and the gender ratio has remained nearly flat.

That discrepancy is invisible in a table — and immediately obvious in the choropleth.

[pause 1 sec]

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
SLIDE 5 — Statistical Charts: Enrollment & Degrees  (~30 sec)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Clicking on any country or institution opens a detail modal with several chart types.

The Enrollment Trends chart uses stacked bars to separate first-year students
from returning cohorts — which makes retention patterns visible over time.

Next to it, the Degrees Awarded chart tracks total degrees per year as a line chart,
showing the academic output trajectory of a country.

At the bottom, we also surface institution-level data from the ETER registry —
showing enrollment and graduate counts for individual universities
over time, which is especially useful for smaller institutions.

[pause 1 sec]

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
SLIDE 6 — Statistical Charts: Gender Analysis  (~30 sec)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The gender analysis view plots BSc and MSc female share on the same
axis, with separate lines — so you can immediately see whether the gap differs
between programme levels within the same country.

And with the comparison modal, you can overlay two countries at once —
four lines total, with colour distinguishing degree level and line style
distinguishing the country.

The key insight here is that MSc female share is consistently higher than BSc
in most countries — yet both remain well below 30%.

[pause 1 sec]

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
SLIDE 7 — Statistical Charts: European Context 1  (~25 sec)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Zooming out to the European level, users can view two comparative charts.

The doughnut chart shows each country's proportional share of the total
European informatics student population.

And the Absolute Scale bar chart shows raw enrollment numbers per country —
where large nations like Germany, the UK, and Turkey naturally dominate.

[pause 1 sec]

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
SLIDE 8 — Statistical Charts: European Context 2  (~25 sec)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

But when you normalize by population — dividing by million inhabitants —
the ranking reverses completely.

Sweden, Finland, and Greece rise to the top, while Germany and Turkey
drop toward the bottom.

And on the right, the side-by-side comparison chart lets you overlay
two countries year by year — making it easy to benchmark any two
nations directly against each other.

[pause 1 sec]

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
SLIDE 9 — Key Findings  (~30 sec)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Let me highlight three things the platform makes visible.

First: the gender gap is persistent. Most European countries have
been below 25% female at BSc level for the entire 14-year window
— and that has barely changed.

Second: growth masks stagnation. Raw female enrollment numbers grew,
giving the impression of progress — but the relative share shows almost
no movement.

Third: population normalization reverses the country rankings completely.
Germany and Turkey dominate the raw charts; Sweden, Finland, and Estonia
lead the per-million view.

[pause 1 sec]

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
SLIDE 10 — Conclusion & Future Work  (~20 sec)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

To summarize: we built a fully static, browser-based platform that brings
geographic, temporal, and comparative visualization to a dataset that
previously required manual spreadsheet work to navigate.

Looking ahead, this app would benefit from automated data refresh via CI/CD,
a year-by-year choropleth animation, an institution-type filter,
a mobile-optimized layout, and export functionality for charts and data.

Thank you for watching.

[END — total target: ~4:00]
